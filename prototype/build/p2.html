The quiet race to build Africa's first continental payments rail

By Meridian Desk, 6 min read

In brief
  • Three fintechs are now competing for one cross-border standard.
  • Central banks are watching, and two have signalled support.
  • The winner could cut remittance costs by a third.

Sending money between two West African countries still takes a detour. A payment from Accra to Abidjan often passes through a bank in Europe or the United States before it lands, adding days and fees that small businesses feel most.

Three fintech companies now want to remove that detour. Each is building a shared network that lets banks and mobile money providers settle with one another directly, in local currencies, without a foreign intermediary.

The stakes are practical. Freight firms such as Kora Freight pay truck operators in three currencies, and every day a payment sits in transit is a day an operator waits for income. Ama Boateng, who founded Kora, has described settlement delays as the quiet tax on cross-border trade.

Central banks hold the deciding vote. Two have signalled support for a common standard, but none has committed to a date, and a network without regulator backing cannot carry real volume.

The winner will likely be decided by distribution rather than technology. Banks will connect to the network their customers already use, which favors companies that have spent years signing up merchants.
